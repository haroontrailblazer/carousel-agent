# Cover Style - First Page of Every Carousel

The selected design's cover layout controls its font, colors, sizes, alignment,
logo, handle and element positions. This application serves any creator or
brand. Never impose a particular website palette, account or favicon.

## Format and source media

- 1080 x 1350 px, portrait 4:5.
- Use a sourced 4-15 second video under the runtime duration contract. A sourced
  still with a restrained push-in is the fallback. Cover media is never AI-generated.
- Prefer the announcement, product, event, paper figure or trusted report's
  actual subject. Keep one recognizable focal point when cropping full-bleed.

## Composition

1. Fill the cover edge to edge with the sourced image or trimmed clip.
2. Apply the mandatory black lower shadow: fade from 48% to fully black at
   66% of the canvas, with solid black below. This matches the design editor.
3. Render the plan's hook_title and its verbatim hook_highlight using the
   cover's configured font, title size, position, alignment and exact colors.
4. Add the selected logo and exact handle only when enabled for the cover.
   Contain the logo in its saved box. Left-align and vertically center the
   handle in its own box. Do not replace saved boxes with fixed rail positions.

Keep these layers simple: media, black shadow, title, logo and handle. Do not
add extra grids, arrows, badges, secondary identities or a slide number. The
cover and CTA are unnumbered; body numbering starts at 01.

## Title rules

The cover is the only slide most people will ever see. A stranger scrolls
past it in under two seconds. In that time the title, together with the
picture, has to tell them what happened and why they should care. A title
that only reports a fact is not a hook. It needs a stake.

### The stranger test (the one rule that decides)

Imagine someone who has never heard of this company or product. From the
cover alone, can they answer both of these?

1. What happened?
2. Why should I care, or why is that surprising?

If either answer is "no idea", rewrite. Most failed covers pass the first
question and fail the second.

### Lead with the stake, not the stat

- A number earns the cover only when a stranger instantly knows what is
  being counted and whether it is good or bad. "REFUSED 2 OF 100" fails:
  2 of 100 what, and is that good? Say the side that carries the stake:
  "SAID YES TO 98 UNSAFE ORDERS".
- A speed, price or size means nothing without a comparison. "33 MS" is just
  a number. Say what it replaces or beats, or say what it lets people do.
- When the facts have no number that passes this test, leave numbers out.
  A clear consequence beats a confusing statistic every time.

### Build a contrast

The pull of a hook is the gap between what people assume and what actually
happened. Find that gap in the facts and put it on the cover.

- Assumed: every AI app needs a big model call. Happened: a small local
  model makes the same yes or no call. Hook: "YOUR APP DOES NOT NEED GPT FOR
  YES OR NO".
- Assumed: AI models refuse dangerous requests. Happened: in robot tests
  they mostly did not. Hook: "GPT-6 SAID YES TO 98 UNSAFE ROBOT ORDERS".

State the surprising side plainly. The reader should feel the gap without
being told "you won't believe this".

### Make it about people

- Say who it affects: developers, your job, your phone, your code, your
  data. "You" and "your" are welcome when the story really touches the
  reader.
- Name the real subject (company, product, model, person) when the name is
  known. When it is not (a new startup, a lab name nobody knows), describe it
  by what it is ("A TINY LOCAL MODEL") and spend the words on the stake.
  Never "the new update", "this AI tool", "a major breakthrough".

### Sound like a person

- Read it aloud. If you would not say it to a friend over coffee, rewrite.
- Use everyday verbs. Newsroom verbs like BREACHED, AIDED, UNVEILS, SLASHES
  and TOUTS sound like a headline machine. Say "broke into", "helped",
  "launched", "cut".
- A direct line to the reader is fine when it is true and useful
  ("STOP PAYING GPT FOR YES OR NO"). A question is fine only when the answer
  is on slide 2 and the question already carries the stake.
- No hype adjectives, no mystery hooks that hide the subject, no unsupported
  claims, no emoji, no hashtags, no em dashes. Only a comma or a period as
  punctuation.
- Never these shapes: A GAME CHANGER, THE FUTURE IS HERE, HERE IS WHY,
  YOU WON'T BELIEVE, EVERYTHING CHANGES NOW, THE TRUTH ABOUT X,
  WHAT NOBODY IS TELLING YOU.

### Match the picture

The title and the cover picture are read together. The title must not just
label what the picture already shows. It adds what the picture cannot: the
consequence, the number with its meaning, the surprise.

### Length is a readability rule

- Up to 9 words, and aim for 40 characters or fewer. That fills up to three
  thick lines while keeping the type large.
- Past that the renderer shrinks the type to make it fit, and a shrunk hook
  is an unread hook. Characters matter more than words: count the letters.
- Do not cut the stake to save characters. Cut filler words first.

### Write three, ship one

Draft three different hooks, each built on a different angle: the
consequence, the contrast, and the reader's own stake. Run each through the
stranger test. Ship the one a stranger would understand fastest AND care
about most. If two tie, ship the shorter. Never ship the first line you
thought of, and never ship a bare "SUBJECT VERB NUMBER" stat line.

### Weak to strong

    Stat, no stake:  GPT-6 ASTRA REFUSED 2 OF 100      -> 2 of 100 what? good or bad?
    Strong:          GPT-6 SAID YES TO 98 UNSAFE ROBOT ORDERS

    Number, no meaning: LAYA ANSWERS LOCALLY IN 33 MS  -> who is Laya, why care?
    Strong:             YOUR APP DOES NOT NEED GPT FOR YES OR NO

    Newsroom verb:  COMPANY BREACHED 3 REAL FIRMS      -> who did what to whom?
    Strong:         HACKERS USED AI TO BREAK INTO 3 COMPANIES

    Vague:  A MAJOR AI UPDATE ARRIVES                   -> no subject, no change
    Strong: OPENAI CUT GPT-5 PRICES 80%

    Hyped:  AN ABSOLUTELY INSANE NEW BREAKTHROUGH       -> says nothing
    Strong: THIS MODEL RUNS ON YOUR LAPTOP

### Highlight

- The highlight must be a verbatim substring of the title.
- Choose the two to four word payoff: the stake, the surprising side of the
  contrast, or the number together with what it counts ("98 UNSAFE ROBOT
  ORDERS", not "98"). Highlight the part that makes the reader stop, never a
  connecting phrase.
- Do not alter an approved title unless the current review requests it.

## Identity and visual treatment

Use the design's uploaded logo and handle, with the run's account as fallback.
Hidden marks and unbranded runs are valid. Reference media and historical
feedback never supply a substitute identity. Do not tint factual source media
with an unrelated palette or add floating logos, glows or fake evidence.

The cover is composed in code. It does not depend on an external overlay file
or an old brand's reference assets.
